# Data Experience Design Playbook

This playbook records reusable design knowledge developed while creating and testing educational data-science experiences.

It exists so that useful decisions do not remain trapped in individual applications, conversation history or the memory of one developer.

## How to use it

The playbook sits between broad educational principles and the design of an individual resource.

Use the pattern:

**shared playbook → local resource design → explicit deviations**

The playbook records what is currently known about recurring design problems. Individual resources decide whether a shared decision applies in their own scientific, educational and delivery context.

Do not mechanically copy pedagogy, provenance or contributor histories between resources.

## Decisions

`decisions/` contains established working decisions.

A decision record should normally explain:

- the design problem;
- the current decision;
- why that decision was made;
- important boundaries or failure modes;
- reusable vocabulary or patterns where useful;
- circumstances that should trigger reconsideration.

Decisions may be revised when evidence from new resources, learners, facilitators or teachers shows that the current approach is inadequate.

## Templates

`../templates/` contains short reusable workflows for applying playbook decisions.

Templates are deliberately lighter than the full decision records. Use them for routine work. Return to the relevant decision record when the answer is uncertain, contested or needs adaptation.

## Local resources remain authoritative for local decisions

A topic-specific resource should own its:

- audience and prior knowledge;
- scientific question;
- dataset and provenance;
- learning intentions;
- learner reasoning journey;
- pedagogical sequence;
- contributors and intellectual lineage;
- resource-specific implementation decisions.

Shared design knowledge should make those decisions easier to make, not replace them.
