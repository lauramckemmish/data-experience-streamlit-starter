# Contributor credit

**Status:** Established working decision  
**Scope:** Educational data-science resources built from this starter  
**Review:** Revisit when a new resource exposes a case this model does not handle well.

## Purpose

Contributor credits should show the distinctive perspectives and intellectual contributions that materially shaped a resource or the approach behind it.

They are not intended to record all labour, participation, delivery, job titles, seniority or professional status.

## Decision

Credit people for:

- a distinctive perspective that materially influenced the resource;
- an intellectual contribution that shaped the learning approach;
- relevant intellectual or educational lineage from which the resource grew.

Do not automatically credit:

- delivery alone;
- attendance or participation;
- amount of work;
- seniority or job title;
- generic expertise that did not materially affect the resource.

If delivery led to observations or reasoning that changed the resource, credit the resulting perspective or intellectual contribution rather than the act of delivery itself.

## Ordering and presentation

- List named contributors alphabetically by surname.
- Use short contribution labels, normally around 2–4 words.
- Describe the contribution, not the contributor's position or status.
- A person may have one or more labels when genuinely warranted.
- Different numbers of labels do not imply different levels of importance.
- Prefer a small shared vocabulary over inventing a unique description for every contributor.
- Do not use badges or other presentation that makes contribution labels feel like scores.

## Current contribution vocabulary

### Research translation

Turning authentic research practices, questions, data, computational methods or ways of reasoning into learning experiences.

### Data-science perspective

Bringing a specifically data-science way of understanding, structuring or reasoning about the activity.

### Pedagogical expertise

Bringing expertise in learning design, educational evaluation and how learning experiences work.

### Teacher perspective

Judgement grounded in substantial direct secondary-school classroom teaching experience.

### Near-peer perspective

Insight grounded in relative proximity to the learner journey and transition toward more independent or facilitating practice.

## Vocabulary rule

This is a working vocabulary, not a closed taxonomy.

Reuse an existing term when it accurately describes a contribution. Add a new term only when a new resource reveals a genuinely different contribution that the existing vocabulary cannot represent accurately.

## Resource-specific provenance

Contributor credit and intellectual lineage must be established separately for each resource.

Do not copy contributor lists or provenance from another experience merely because resources share software, pedagogy or project history.

For example, a contribution that forms part of the intellectual lineage of an exoplanet resource may not form part of the provenance of an animal-traits resource.

## Wider participation

Named intellectual credits do not need to enumerate everyone who has participated in delivery or testing.

Where appropriate, separately acknowledge the wider facilitators, teachers, students and others whose observations and feedback have informed continuing development.
